abs-risk

Consumer credit risk from public SEC filings. Every number traces to a filing.

American Express loses less than Synchrony. Toyota loses less than Santander. Both hold at the same credit score. Either those issuers picked people who do not fall behind, or the same people fall behind and pay that account first. The loss rate cannot tell the two apart, and almost every public comparison stops there.

The answer, and how it was reached
Splitting losses into falling behind and falling behind becoming a write-off separates the two stories. It is selection, in cards and in auto loans, and the cycle test agrees. What that rules out, and what it cannot. Card trusts
554 monthly reports from six master trusts, December 2018 to July 2026. Charge-off split into entry, progression and conversion; residuals against what each pool's score mix predicts under four published loss curves. Auto loans, loan by loan
759,808 loans across ten deals and seven lenders. Cumulative charge-off by score bucket with prepayment as a competing risk, a hazard model for lender and loan structure, and regression discontinuity at lenders' own pricing cutoffs.

Source and methods: github.com/SamuelJWebber/abs-risk. Data: SEC EDGAR forms 10-D, 424B and ABS-EE, and CFPB Consumer Credit Card Market Report figure data.
