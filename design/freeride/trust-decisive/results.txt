--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(2b) THE CONFOUND, STATED FIRST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     Amex's payment rate is ~0.447 and Synchrony's is ~0.233. Almost none of that gap is
     priority. It is PRODUCT DESIGN:
       - Amex's securitised pool is dominated by charge-card-like and pay-in-full revolving
         behaviour; a transactor repays ~100% of the balance every month by contract, not by
         choice under stress.
       - Synchrony is private-label/retail with deferred-interest promotional balances that are
         DESIGNED not to amortise until the promo window ends.
     A level difference of 2x in payment rate is therefore uninformative about priority. Every
     test below is on CHANGES (elasticities of log payment rate to the common cycle), which
     differences out any time-invariant product feature. A trust-specific TREND in product mix
     is NOT differenced out; that is the residual threat and it is not testable here.

     Quantifying the level confound directly is not possible from these files: no trust prints a
     transactor/revolver split, a pay-in-full share, or a promotional-balance share. The best
     available proxy is portfolio yield (finance charge income / receivables): a pool of
     transactors earns little interest.

    trust  mean_payment_rate  mean_yield  yield_per_unit_pr
     amex             0.4474      0.2773             0.6199
     bofa             0.2451      0.1878             0.7661
    chase             0.4515      0.2244             0.4970
     citi             0.3421      0.2071             0.6054
    comet             0.4351      0.2610             0.5999
synchrony             0.2332      0.2726             1.1690

     Amex has the HIGHEST payment rate and a yield (0.277) barely below Synchrony's (0.273).
     If Amex's pool were mostly transactors its yield would be far lower. So the Amex payment
     rate is high for reasons the yield does not explain, and 'transactor mix' is a partial, not
     a complete, account of the level. Either way the level is not evidence of priority.

(2e) DENOMINATORS - what each trust's printed payment rate actually divides by
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     amex       principal collections / MONTHLY PAYMENT RATE implies BEGINNING TOTAL RECEIVABLES (principal + finance charge). Denominator NOT printed; inferred.
     bofa       Collections of Principal Receivables / PRIOR MONTH PRINCIPAL RECEIVABLES.
     chase      Principal Payment Rate: principal collections / AVERAGE POOL BALANCE (printed, verified).
     citi       4. Principal Payment Rate, on AVERAGE PRINCIPAL over the due period (due-period days vary; trust also prints a separate Total Payment Rate).
     comet      principal collections / ADJUSTED BEGINNING PRINCIPAL, which INCLUDES additional principal receivables added that month (an inflow in the denominator).
     synchrony  principal collections / BOP PRINCIPAL RECEIVABLES.

     CROSS-DENOMINATOR FLAGS:
       amex vs everyone: amex is the ONLY trust whose denominator is TOTAL receivables rather
         than PRINCIPAL receivables. Total > principal, so amex's printed rate is biased DOWN
         relative to a principal-basis rate by roughly the finance-charge share of receivables
         (~3% of the balance). This makes amex's LEVEL understated, not overstated - it does not
         manufacture the amex/synchrony level gap, it shrinks it.
       amex denominator is not printed at all; it is inferred from collections / printed rate.
       citi's due period is not a calendar month (due_period_days varies), so citi's rate has a
         day-count wobble the others do not.
       comet's denominator includes same-month additions, so a month with a big pool addition
         mechanically DEPRESSES comet's payment rate. Same denominator-dilution mechanism the
         referee flagged for Synchrony's Dec-2019 delinquency share.
       LEVEL comparisons across trusts are therefore not interpretable. ELASTICITIES are, so
         long as the denominator definition is constant within a trust over time - which the
         row labels confirm it is (identical label in the first and last month for all six).
       Residual caveat: a denominator that is constant in DEFINITION can still move for
         mechanical reasons (comet's additions), which is noise in that trust's elasticity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(2g) CHASING THE ONE SIGNIFICANT RESULT - is the first-difference effect a cycle or a calendar?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

     The candidate finding: in month-to-month changes, when the market's 30+ rises, Amex's
     payment rate falls LESS than Synchrony's (+0.58, z 3.3) and Amex's 30+ rises LESS than
     Synchrony's (-0.52, z -2.8). Both are the priority direction. Three ways it could be fake:
       (i)  SEASONALITY / DAY COUNT. February has 28 days and fewer collection days, so every
            trust's payment rate dips and its 30+ jumps in a fixed monthly pattern. Trusts with
            different due-period conventions get hit by different amounts, so a seasonal
            difference would load onto the differenced regressor with no behaviour involved.
       (ii) TIMING / LEAD-LAG. The six trusts do not close their due periods on the same day.
            If Amex's series is half a month out of phase with the common series, the
            contemporaneous difference regression picks up the phase, and the SUM of the
            coefficients on the lead, the contemporaneous term and the lag goes back to zero.
       (iii) A REAL short-run priority response that genuinely dies out within a year.
     Tests: add month-of-year dummies; run the distributed lag and report the SUM; and run the
     12-month difference, which is immune to fixed seasonality but keeps cyclical movement.

     VERDICT ON THE FIRST-DIFFERENCE RESULT: IT IS A PHASE ARTEFACT, CONCLUSIVELY.
     Payment rate, distributed lag: lead -0.799, contemporaneous +1.399, lag -0.602. The
     contemporaneous coefficient is enormous and it is flanked on BOTH sides by large negative
     ones. Their SUM is -0.002 with se 0.106 - a zero to three decimal places. That is exactly
     what you get when two series carry the same information a fraction of a month out of
     phase. The 12-month difference, which is immune to fixed seasonality and to phase but
     keeps every bit of cyclical movement, gives -0.011 (se 0.045, z -0.25). Dead.
     30+ ratio, same story: contemporaneous -0.899 flanked by +0.305 and +0.327, sum -0.267
     (se 0.173, z -1.54, p 0.12); the 12-month difference is +0.161 (se 0.221, z +0.73).
     Nothing survives once you stop asking about a single month's timing.

     THE SQUEEZE-ONLY RESULT DOES NOT COLLAPSE, BUT IT CONTRADICTS ITSELF.
     Inside 2023-07..2026-07 the common stressor X has sd 0.0500, and a linear trend plus
     eleven month-of-year dummies explain R2 = 0.605 of it - so roughly 61% of what
     little variation there is is calendar, not credit. What is left gives:
       payment rate: +0.159 (z 1.28, ns) with month dummies, +0.279 (z 2.07) adding a trend
                     -> WEAK, PRIORITY DIRECTION (Amex's payment rate holds up better).
       30+ ratio   : +0.692 (z 3.09) with month dummies, +0.735 (z 2.99) adding a trend
                     -> AGAINST PRIORITY. A POSITIVE coefficient here means that when stress
                        rises inside the squeeze, Amex's delinquency rises MORE than
                        Synchrony's, i.e. the gap COMPRESSES - the opposite of protection.
     THE TWO MEASURES POINT OPPOSITE WAYS IN THE SAME WINDOW. A real priority mechanism cannot
     simultaneously make Amex pay better AND go delinquent faster. This is 37 monthly
     observations with 13 regressors, leaving 24 degrees of freedom, and Newey-West 6-lag
     standard errors are not trustworthy at that length. I read the pair as noise, and I would
     read it as noise whichever way it had come out - which is the point of saying so here
     rather than after seeing the sign I liked.

========================================================================================================
VERDICT
========================================================================================================

ONE SENTENCE: No - nothing in the pool-level data distinguishes priority from selection, because
the only two tests with any direct power (the payment-rate elasticity and the no-degrees-of-freedom
log-ratio regression) both return null differences between Amex and Synchrony with confidence
intervals wide enough to contain effects several times larger than the entire gap being explained;
what WOULD distinguish them is borrower-level data in which the same household holds both cards -
a credit-bureau tradeline panel, the CFPB CCDB, or the NY Fed CCP - where you can watch which
account a single squeezed borrower pays first.

Supporting numbers, each labelled by what kind of claim it is:

  (a) PROVEN BY ARITHMETIC ON THE DATA
      - 5 duplicate trust-months, 10 rows, all identical on every data column; 554 -> 549 rows.
      - The aligned panel is 89 months, not 87.
      - Dedupe changes neither the raw nor the mix-adjusted Amex/Synchrony ratio, because the
        duplicates are bofa/citi/comet only and the pinning level cancels out of the ratio.
      - Synchrony's receivables rose +38.6% in Dec-2019; the pre-stimulus baseline is contaminated.
      - The six cyclical betas average to exactly 1 by construction, so a flat log ratio is the
        mechanical default under BOTH stories.

  (b) ESTIMATED, WITH A MODEL AND UNCERTAINTY
      - Payment-rate elasticity gap, Amex minus Synchrony: +0.0281 (se 0.0870, z +0.32, p 0.746).
      - No-DoF delinquency-ratio elasticity: +0.0813 (se 0.1495, z +0.54, p 0.587).
      - MDE: an effect smaller than 34% of the ratio per doubling of market stress is invisible.
      - Honest mix-adjusted range: 0.31 .. 0.98.
      - Stimulus contrast: sign flips with the baseline, none significant.

      - First-difference 'finding' (PR +0.58 z 3.3, 30+ -0.52 z -2.8) dissolves: the
        distributed-lag SUM is -0.002 (se 0.106) and the 12-month difference is -0.011
        (se 0.045). A phase artefact.
      - Squeeze-window-only regressions survive the calendar but CONTRADICT each other:
        payment rate +0.159 (priority direction, ns), 30+ ratio +0.692 (against priority, z 3.1).

  (c) SPECULATION, LABELLED AS SUCH
      - That Amex's payment-rate level reflects transactor share rather than priority is a story
        consistent with the yield numbers but not established by them.
      - That a bureau-level test would find a halo is a hypothesis; this analysis cannot support it.

WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY SETTLE IT, in descending order of decisiveness:
  1. A borrower-level tradeline panel where the SAME household holds an Amex and a Synchrony
     private-label card. Condition on the household, then watch which account goes 30 days late
     first when income falls. Household fixed effects hold selection constant BY CONSTRUCTION,
     which is the one thing six aggregate time series can never do. Sources: NY Fed CCP
     (5% anonymised Equifax panel), Experian/TransUnion tradeline extracts, the CFPB CCDB.
  2. Failing that, a within-trust event with borrower-level randomness in the STRESSOR but not
     in the pool: e.g. the state-by-state expiry of pandemic UI supplements, or a
     geographically concentrated layoff, compared across trusts whose pools are geographically
     disclosed. The prospectuses do print state concentration; that is a real, un-run test on
     data that exists, and it would need state-level unemployment merged in.
  3. Failing that, a common score scale. If any one of these trusts published BOTH a FICO and a
     VantageScore distribution for the same pool in the same month, the 0.31-0.98 mix range
     would collapse to something narrow enough to be worth arguing about. Nothing in these
     files does that, and no amount of re-analysis of these files can create it.

WHAT WOULD NOT SETTLE IT: any further regression of these six monthly series on each other.
The design is exhausted. The betas average to one by construction, the stressor's whole
observed range is a 2.17x swing, and the minimum detectable effect is ~34% of the very gap
under investigation. More specifications will only find more sign flips.
